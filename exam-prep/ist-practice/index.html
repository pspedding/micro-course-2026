    🎯 In-Semester Test (IST) Practice

    ECON1001 Introductory Microeconomics — USYD Semester 1 2026

    About the IST

    The In-Semester Test is a 20-question multiple-choice exam covering Weeks 1–8 (Modules 1–8). You have approximately 45 minutes. Questions test both conceptual understanding and numerical problem-solving.

    How to use this page

    • Complete the 2026 IST Version A under timed conditions (45 min) before checking answers
    • Use the answer key at the bottom to mark your work
    • For any incorrect answers, revisit the relevant module lessons and quizzes

    2026 IST — Version A

    Instructions: Choose the best answer for each question.

    Q1. A country produces only two goods: food and clothing. If the economy is operating inside its production possibilities frontier, this implies that:

    • A. Resources are fully and efficiently used
    • B. It is impossible to produce more of both goods
    • C. Some resources are unemployed or misallocated
    • D. The opportunity cost of both goods is zero

    Q2. Indonesia can produce either 10 motorcycles or 20 cars per day. Sweden can produce either 6 motorcycles or 6 cars per day. Which country has a comparative advantage in producing motorcycles, and which in cars?

    • A. Indonesia in both cars and motorcycles
    • B. Sweden in both cars and motorcycles
    • C. Indonesia in cars and Sweden in motorcycles
    • D. Sweden in cars and Indonesia in motorcycles

    Q3. 8 identical firms have individual supply curves: q_s = 5P − 10. Market demand: Q_d = 120 − 10P. What is the total quantity supplied at equilibrium?

    • A. 0
    • B. 4
    • C. 33.33
    • D. 60
    • E. 80

    Q4. If a firm experiences diminishing marginal product of labour and labour is the only variable input, then it must be that:

    • A. Total output falls as more labour is hired
    • B. Average total cost is rising
    • C. Marginal cost is rising
    • D. Average variable cost is falling

    Q5. The new Sydney Metro reduces commute time from 1.5 hours to 0.5 hours, and increases trips per year from 2 million to 3 million. If the value of time is $20/hour, the increase in consumer surplus for new commuters is:

    • A. $60 million
    • B. $50 million
    • C. $40 million
    • D. $20 million
    • E. $10 million

    Q6. Which graph shows a price elasticity of demand equal to infinity?

    • A. Vertical line
    • B. Horizontal line
    • C. Upward-sloping line
    • D. Downward-sloping line

    Q7. Which section of the MC curve best represents the short-run supply curve of a perfectly competitive firm? (Select the option showing the MC curve above the minimum of AVC)

    • A. The entire MC curve
    • B. The section of MC above minimum AVC
    • C. The section of MC above minimum ATC
    • D. The downward-sloping section of MC

    Q8. Market price decreases and quantity decreases. Which is the most likely cause?

    • A. An increase in demand
    • B. A decrease in demand
    • C. An increase in supply
    • D. A decrease in supply

    Q9. Income rises. For an inferior good, what happens to demand?

    • A. Demand increases (shifts right)
    • B. Demand decreases (shifts left)
    • C. Demand is unaffected
    • D. A movement up along the demand curve

    Q10. Individual 1: Q = −3P + 6. Individual 2: Q = −4P + 4. At what price is there a kink in the market demand curve?

    • A. 1
    • B. 2
    • C. 3
    • D. 4
    • E. 5

    Q11. A perfectly competitive firm faces a market price above its ATC minimum. What happens next in the long run?

    • A. Existing firms raise their price
    • B. Existing firms lower their price
    • C. Consumers demand fewer units
    • D. Existing firms exit the market
    • E. New firms enter the market

    Q12. Market demand: Q = 100 − 2P. The elasticity of demand is −0.25. What is the quantity demanded?

    • A. 10
    • B. 50
    • C. 80
    • D. 100
    • E. 120

    Q13. Which of the following would cause a rightward shift in the demand curve for coffee?

    • A. An increase in the price of coffee
    • B. An increase in the price of tea (a substitute)
    • C. A decrease in consumers' income, if coffee is a normal good
    • D. An improvement in coffee-making technology

    Q14. In trade based on comparative advantage, both parties gain because:

    • A. Working together makes each person more productive
    • B. Trading allows each person to access the other's product
    • C. One person is more productive, raising average productivity
    • D. Exchange allows people to specialise in what they produce more cheaply

    Q15. A subsidy for children's sports results in a very small increase in participation. This suggests:

    • A. Highly inelastic demand for children's sports
    • B. Highly elastic demand for children's sports
    • C. Children's sports is a normal good
    • D. Children's sports is an inferior good

    Q16. Market demand: Q_d = 10 − 2P. Supply defined by MC = 4 (perfectly elastic). What is the producer surplus?

    • A. 0
    • B. 1
    • C. 5.56
    • D. 8
    • E. 9

    Q17. A firm faces demand Q = 10 − 2P. If price rises from P = 1 to P = 2, what is the change in total revenue?

    • A. 0
    • B. 2
    • C. 4
    • D. 6
    • E. 8

    Q18. Short-run cost: TC(q) = 5 + q + q². What is average variable cost when q = 5?

    • A. 7.8
    • B. 7
    • C. 4/5
    • D. 5
    • E. 6

    Q19. TC(q) = 6 + 2q + q²; MC = 2 + 2q. Market price = 6. What is profit at the profit-maximising output?

    • A. −2
    • B. 0
    • C. 1
    • D. 2
    • E. 4

    Q20. If the market price is above the equilibrium price, then:

    • A. Quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied
    • B. There is excess demand
    • C. There is excess supply
    • D. Price will tend to rise further

    💡 Key Worked Examples

    Q5 — New Commuter Surplus (Half-Rule)

    New commuters travel 1 million extra trips. Their surplus = ½ × (time saving per trip) × (new trips): - Time saving = 1.5 − 0.5 = 1 hour/trip at \(20/hr = **\)20 value/trip - New trips = 3m − 2m = 1 million - New commuter CS = ½ × \(20 × 1,000,000 = **\)10 million → Answer: E

    Q12 — Elasticity Calculation

    ε = (dQ/dP) × (P/Q) = −2 × (P/Q) = −0.25 → −2P/Q = −0.25 → P/Q = 0.125 → P = 0.125Q Substitute into demand: Q = 100 − 2(0.125Q) = 100 − 0.25Q 1.25Q = 100 → Q = 80 → Answer: C

    Q18 — AVC Calculation

    TC = 5 + q + q²; Variable cost = TC − Fixed cost = q + q² At q=5: VC = 5 + 25 = 30; AVC = 30/5 = 6 → Answer: E

    Q19 — Profit Maximisation

    P = MC: 6 = 2 + 2q → q = 2 TR = 6 × 2 = 12; TC = 6 + 2(2) + 2² = 6 + 4 + 4 = 14 Profit = 12 − 14 = −2 → Answer: A
